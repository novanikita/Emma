Voice agent market research

Emma

A library of findings for a product that will complement the Diptalk ecosystem. What Emma shares with Diptalk is speech, and signals above the transcript. Emma is aimed at conversation, and at understanding one person: her interlocutor. The point is the quality of conversation as a process; solving tasks follows from that.

Principles

Starting hypotheses. We will refine this list as the research goes on.

  1. Small startups can offer higher-quality voice agents than OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, and other large models.
  2. Emma’s value is in empathy.
  3. Emma’s value is in focusing on the process, not the outcome.

4 Full-duplex spontaneous dialogue models: synchronization and turn-taking prediction research Pablo Riera, Pablo Brusco et al. (independent researchers, based on the Moshi model) 2026-05-19 science The paper studies how full-duplex spoken dialogue models (able to listen and speak simultaneously) synchronize internal representations during conversation, using the Moshi model. Through Centered Kernel Alignment, the authors show strong synchronization of interlocutors' representations on a clean channel (peaking near zero lag, degrading with noise), and through causal LSTMs, that the model's internal state encodes turn-switching signals in advance — the model "anticipates" the moment to take or yield the floor. A direct hit on the "speech recognition, turn-taking" criterion. Academic work, not a product. Shows that turn-taking is solved not by rules but by analyzing the model's hidden states — a direction useful for Emma's architecture.

29 "Did the caller go silent or just pause to think?": how a contact center cut AI-assistant interruptions from 14% to 3.2% product, success outsourced contact center (engineer, handle Innesiya) 2026-08-19 vendor An engineering team at an outsourced contact center describes how they taught their own voice stack to tell a customer's thinking pause apart from the end of an utterance. The solution was a four-layer pipeline: a fast acoustic silence detector + incremental ASR with intermediate hypotheses + an utterance-completeness classifier built on rubert-tiny2 (29M parameters) + an adaptive timeout based on completion probability. It drew attention as one of the most concrete RU-market stories specifically about turn-taking recognition, with clear before/after numbers. Results: premature speech interruptions dropped from 14% to 3.2%, average response latency fell from 1200ms to 480ms, and the share of calls escalated to a live operator due to a bot failure fell from 27% to 16%. Evidence level: production, real phone traffic, not a demo. Market language is Russian; a mid-sized contact center.
