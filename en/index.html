Emma

A library of findings for Emma, a product that will complement the Modulate ecosystem. What matters for her is the quality of conversation as a process; solving tasks follows from that.

Hypotheses

Starting hypotheses. We will refine this list as the research goes on.

  1. Small startups can offer higher-quality voice agents than OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, and other large models.
  2. Emma’s value is in empathy.
  3. Emma’s value is in focusing on the process, not the outcome.
  4. Emma’s character can be chosen.
  5. A product that patches today’s shortcomings of the large models breaks on the day of their next update. The winner is the one betting on the models getting better: even if the integration is clumsy today and the agent speaks poorly, every new version of the base model makes the product better on its own, with no retraining on our side.
  6. Emma is a harness and orchestration, not a model.
  7. On one channel Emma carries out the task and holds the conversation; on a second one she reflects on what is happening and changes the behaviour of the first channel.
  8. During a conversation, Emma should give signals — the way Deep Talk does.
  9. Emma’s memory should be open to viewing and possibly editing — a principle of transparency and the tool’s “delicacy.”
